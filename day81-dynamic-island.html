The Dynamic Island — a priority queue wearing a rounded rectangle, and the corner radius that gives the whole trick away.

One shape, four sizes, and never a decision left to chance

The island is a single black rounded rectangle that changes size. What makes it feel alive is not the animation — it is that two things can want it at once, and the rules for who gets it are fixed, total and deterministic. A timer finishing does not fight a phone call. The call wins, every time, and the timer waits its turn.

The corner radius is not a style, it is a constraint

Pick a fixed radius and animate the box from the small pill to the tall expanded card, and the corners visibly change character — the shape stops reading as the same object and starts reading as a box that grew. The island keeps its corners continuous with the screen's own corner, which means the radius has to be a function of the height, not a constant.

Every transition the machine allows, and every one it refuses

Four presentations. Sixteen ordered pairs. The machine permits — of them in one step, and the refusals are the interesting half: you cannot go from minimal straight to expanded, because nothing in a minimal presentation is big enough to be tapped. A resolver that names a far-away target does not get to teleport — the machine routes through the legal hops, and the last column is the route it takes.

Priority, not recency

The naive model is a stack: whatever started last is on top. It is wrong, and it is wrong in a way users notice immediately — start a timer during a call and the call disappears. The real model is a priority queue keyed by the kind of activity, with the start time used only to break ties between two activities of equal rank.

The spring, and why it has to settle exactly

A spring that merely approaches its target is a bug. The island has to arrive at a whole number of points and stop, or the next transition starts from a position that is off by a fraction and the error compounds. The implementation runs a critically damped spring and then snaps once both the distance and the velocity fall under a threshold.

Width and height do not animate independently

Animate them as two separate springs and the island passes through shapes that never existed as a presentation — briefly wide and tall at once, or thin and short. A single scalar t drives both, so every intermediate frame lies on the straight line between two real presentations.
